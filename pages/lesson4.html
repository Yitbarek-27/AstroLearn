ðŸ’¡ What is Light?

Light is a form of energy that travels through space as electromagnetic radiation. The light we see with our eyes is only a small part of a much larger group of waves called the electromagnetic spectrum.

Light travels extremely fast at about 300,000 kilometers per second. Because space is so enormous, astronomers use the time light takes to travel to measure distances in the universe.

Different objects in space produce different types of light. Astronomers use special telescopes designed to detect specific wavelengths.

For example, the James Webb Space Telescope observes mainly infrared light, allowing scientists to see distant galaxies and stars that formed billions of years ago.

When we look at distant stars, we are actually seeing the past because their light takes years, thousands of years, or even millions of years to reach Earth.

Human eyes can only detect visible light, but the universe produces many other forms of radiation. By studying the complete electromagnetic spectrum, scientists can discover hidden features of planets, stars, and galaxies.

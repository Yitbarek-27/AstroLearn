🌌 Lesson 1: What Are Galaxies?

Galaxies are among the largest and most fascinating structures in the universe. They contain enormous numbers of stars, clouds of gas and dust, planets, stellar remnants, and large amounts of dark matter. From beautiful spiral galaxies to giant elliptical galaxies, each galaxy has its own structure and history. In this lesson, we will discover what galaxies are, what they contain, how gravity holds them together, how astronomers study them, and where our own Milky Way fits into the universe.

🌌 What Exactly Is a Galaxy?

A galaxy is a huge system of stars, gas, dust, planets, stellar remnants, and dark matter that is held together mainly by gravity. When we look at a galaxy through a telescope, we may see a beautiful collection of stars and glowing clouds. However, what we see is only a small part of the complete galaxy.

Galaxies can be incredibly large. Their sizes are usually described using light-years, the distance that light travels in one year. Some galaxies are only a few thousand light-years across, while large galaxies can extend across hundreds of thousands of light-years.

The number of stars inside a galaxy can also vary enormously. Some small galaxies contain millions of stars, while large galaxies may contain hundreds of billions of stars. Our Sun is only one star among the enormous population of stars in the Milky Way.

It is important to remember that a galaxy is not simply a group of stars sitting close together. It is a complex system in which stars, gas, dust, and other forms of matter interact with one another over millions and billions of years.

Galaxies are also not isolated from the rest of the universe. They can interact with neighboring galaxies, form groups and clusters, and become part of enormous structures extending across cosmic distances.

⭐ What Is Inside a Galaxy?

Stars are probably the first things you imagine when you think about a galaxy. They are certainly one of its most visible components. However, galaxies contain much more than stars.

A galaxy can contain stars of many different ages and types. Some stars may have formed recently from clouds of gas, while others may be billions of years old. By studying these different stars, astronomers can learn about the history of a galaxy and how it has changed over time.

Galaxies also contain enormous clouds of gas. Hydrogen is particularly important because it provides much of the raw material needed to form new stars. When gravity causes a sufficiently dense region of gas to collapse, a new star can eventually form.

Another important component is cosmic dust. Dust consists of tiny solid particles made from different elements and compounds. Dust can sometimes block visible light, producing dark regions in images of galaxies. However, astronomers can study these regions using infrared and other wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation.

Galaxies also contain the remains of stars that have reached the end of their lives. These include white dwarfs, neutron stars, and black holes. Some galaxies also contain enormous amounts of hot gas surrounding their visible regions.

Planets are another expected component. We know that planets are common in the Milky Way because astronomers have discovered thousands of planets outside our Solar System. Although detecting planets around individual stars in distant galaxies is extremely difficult, planets are expected to exist throughout the universe.

🌑 Dark Matter and Galactic Halos

One of the most mysterious parts of a galaxy is something that astronomers cannot directly see. This substance is called dark matter.

Dark matter does not appear to produce, reflect, or absorb light in the same way ordinary matter does. Because of this, telescopes cannot simply photograph dark matter like they can stars and gas. So how do scientists know it is there?

The main evidence comes from gravity. Astronomers measure how stars and gas move inside galaxies. In many galaxies, objects far from the center are moving much faster than they would be expected to move if the galaxy contained only the visible matter we can observe.

This suggests that there is additional mass producing extra gravitational attraction. Scientists believe that this unseen mass is largely dark matter.

Dark matter appears to form a huge structure called a dark matter halo around many galaxies. The visible stars and gas make up the bright region that we can see, but the dark matter halo can extend much farther into space.

This gives us an important picture of a galaxy: The beautiful stars we see are only part of the complete system. A large amount of the galaxy's mass may exist in a form that we cannot observe directly.

Dark matter is still one of the major unanswered questions in modern astronomy. Scientists continue to study its properties and its role in the formation of galaxies and the large-scale structure of the universe.

🌀 Different Shapes of Galaxies

Galaxies do not all look the same. Astronomers have observed galaxies with many different shapes and structures. These differences provide clues about how galaxies form and evolve.

🌀 Spiral Galaxies

Spiral galaxies have a flattened disk with curved structures called spiral arms extending from their central regions. The spiral arms can contain large amounts of gas and dust, making them important locations for the formation of new stars.

Our own Milky Way is a spiral galaxy. From outside the Milky Way, an observer would see its disk, central region, spiral structure, and surrounding halo.

🔵 Elliptical Galaxies

Elliptical galaxies generally have smoother, rounded, or oval-shaped structures. They can range from relatively small galaxies to enormous giant elliptical galaxies containing huge numbers of stars.

Many elliptical galaxies contain large populations of older stars and relatively little cold gas available for forming new stars.

✨ Irregular Galaxies

Irregular galaxies do not have a simple spiral or elliptical shape. Their unusual appearances can sometimes result from gravitational interactions with other galaxies.

When galaxies pass close to one another, their gravity can distort their shapes. Over extremely long periods of time, galaxies can even merge together.

This means that the shape of a galaxy can tell astronomers something about its past.

⚖️ Gravity Shapes Galaxies

Gravity is one of the most important forces controlling a galaxy. Every object with mass produces gravity, and a galaxy contains an enormous amount of matter. The combined gravitational attraction of this matter keeps the galaxy together and controls the motion of its stars and gas.

Stars in a galaxy are not simply sitting still. They move through space, and many stars orbit around the central region of their galaxy. This is similar to the way planets orbit stars, although the scale is enormously larger.

The gravitational influence of a galaxy also affects objects far outside its bright central regions. The motion of stars and gas gives astronomers valuable information about the distribution of mass inside a galaxy.

Gravity also acts between galaxies. If two galaxies pass close enough to each other, their gravitational fields can affect one another. The shapes of the galaxies may become distorted, and their gas clouds can be disturbed.

In some cases, galaxies can eventually merge. A galaxy merger can take millions or billions of years and can dramatically change the structure of the galaxies involved.

These interactions show that galaxies are not permanent, unchanging objects. They are dynamic systems that evolve throughout cosmic history.

☀️ Our Galaxy: The Milky Way

Our home galaxy is called the Milky Way. It is a large spiral galaxy containing hundreds of billions of stars, including our Sun.

The Solar System is not located at the center of the Milky Way. Instead, it is located within the galaxy's disk, far from the central region.

Because we live inside the Milky Way, our view of the galaxy is very different from what we might see if we could observe it from far outside. We cannot simply take a photograph of the entire Milky Way from outside because Earth and the Solar System are inside the galaxy.

On a dark night away from strong artificial lights, the Milky Way can appear as a faint, cloudy band stretching across the sky. That cloudy appearance is caused by the combined light of enormous numbers of distant stars.

Some parts of the Milky Way appear dark because clouds of dust block visible light. Astronomers use other wavelengths, especially infrared and radio observations, to study regions that are difficult to see in visible light.

At the center of the Milky Way is a supermassive black hole called Sagittarius A*. It has a mass millions of times greater than the mass of the Sun.

Although Sagittarius A* is extremely massive, it is only a tiny part of the overall Milky Way. The galaxy itself extends across enormous distances and contains vast populations of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter.

🔭 How Do Astronomers Study Galaxies?

Galaxies are incredibly distant, so astronomers cannot normally travel to them and collect samples. Instead, scientists learn about galaxies by studying the radiation that reaches Earth and space-based observatories.

Visible light is only one small portion of the electromagnetic spectrum. Astronomers can also observe galaxies using radio waves, infrared radiation, ultraviolet radiation, X-rays, and other wavelengths.

🌈 Different Wavelengths Reveal Different Things

  • Visible light: Can reveal stars, glowing gas, dust structures, and the overall appearance of a galaxy.
  • Infrared: Can help astronomers observe through some dusty regions and study cooler objects and star-forming regions.
  • Radio: Can reveal gas and other structures that may be difficult to detect using visible light.
  • X-rays: Can reveal extremely hot gas and energetic objects such as some black-hole systems.

Astronomers can also use spectroscopy. A spectroscope separates light into different wavelengths, producing a spectrum.

The patterns in a spectrum can reveal information about the chemical elements present in stars and gas, their temperatures, and their motion.

Scientists can also use the Doppler effect to study motion. When an object moves toward or away from us, the wavelengths of its light can shift slightly. By measuring these shifts, astronomers can determine how quickly stars or gas are moving.

⏳ Galaxies Let Us Look Into the Past

One of the most amazing ideas in astronomy is that observing distant objects also means looking into the past.

Light travels at a finite speed. Even though light travels incredibly fast, it still needs time to cross the enormous distances between galaxies and Earth.

Suppose a galaxy is one million light-years away. The light reaching our telescopes today began its journey roughly one million years ago. Therefore, we see that galaxy as it looked approximately one million years in the past.

For galaxies billions of light-years away, this effect becomes even more dramatic. Astronomers can observe galaxies as they existed billions of years ago, when the universe was much younger.

This allows scientists to compare nearby galaxies with distant ones and investigate how galaxies changed throughout cosmic history.

Some distant galaxies appear smaller, younger, and more chaotic than many galaxies nearby. By studying huge numbers of galaxies at different distances, astronomers can build a picture of how galaxies formed and evolved over billions of years.

🌐 Galaxies Are Part of Something Bigger

Galaxies themselves are not the largest structures in the universe. Gravity causes galaxies to gather into larger systems.

A relatively small collection of galaxies can form a galaxy group. Larger collections can form galaxy clusters, which may contain hundreds or even thousands of galaxies.

Groups and clusters are connected to even larger structures. On the largest scales, galaxies are arranged along enormous filaments separated by regions containing relatively few galaxies.

This huge arrangement is known as the cosmic web.

The cosmic web developed over billions of years as gravity caused matter to gather into increasingly large structures. Dark matter played an important role in this process because its gravitational influence helped provide the framework around which ordinary matter could gather.

This means that when astronomers study a single galaxy, they are also studying a small part of a much larger cosmic system.

🔄 Galaxies Are Always Changing

A galaxy may look peaceful and unchanging when viewed through a telescope, but galaxies are actually dynamic systems. Their evolution takes place over enormous periods of time.

New stars can form when clouds of gas collapse under gravity. Stars eventually change as they use their nuclear fuel, and some stars return material to their surroundings when they reach the end of their lives.

That material can later become part of new stars and planets. In this way, galaxies continuously recycle matter.

Galaxies can also interact with one another. A close encounter can distort their shapes, compress gas clouds, and trigger periods of increased star formation.

Eventually, two galaxies can merge into a single larger system. Although a galaxy merger sounds like a sudden event, it can actually take millions or billions of years.

Studying galaxies at different stages of their evolution helps astronomers understand how the universe has changed over time.

💡 Key Idea

A galaxy is much more than a beautiful collection of stars. It is a gigantic, constantly changing system containing stars, gas, dust, planets, stellar remnants, and dark matter.

Gravity controls much of the structure and movement within galaxies, while interactions between galaxies can change their shapes and evolution.

Our own Milky Way is just one galaxy among an enormous number of galaxies in the observable universe.

By studying galaxies, astronomers are not only learning about distant objects. They are also investigating how the universe itself formed, changed, and continues to evolve.

📜 Lesson 2: History of Astronomy

Astronomy is one of the oldest sciences in human history. For thousands of years, people have looked at the sky to understand time, seasons, navigation, and the universe.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand how astronomy began.
  • Identify important ancient civilizations that studied the sky.
  • Explain the contributions of Copernicus and Galileo.
  • Recognize how modern astronomy has evolved.

🏛 Ancient Astronomy

Long before telescopes existed, people observed the sky using only their eyes. Ancient civilizations noticed that the Sun, Moon, and stars followed regular patterns. These observations helped people create calendars, predict seasons, and know the best times for farming.

The Egyptians aligned pyramids with stars, the Babylonians carefully recorded planetary movements, the Maya developed accurate calendars, and Chinese astronomers documented eclipses and comets. Their work became the foundation of astronomy.

⭐ Ancient Greek Contributions

Greek thinkers began explaining the universe using science and mathematics rather than myths. Aristotle believed Earth was the center of the universe, while Ptolemy created a detailed Earth-centered model that remained accepted for centuries.

Although these models were later replaced, Greek astronomers introduced mathematical methods that greatly advanced astronomy.

💡 Did You Know?

The oldest known astronomical records are more than 5,000 years old. Ancient observers tracked the sky without telescopes.

🔭 The Scientific Revolution

In the 1500s, Nicolaus Copernicus proposed that the Sun is at the center of the Solar System. This revolutionary idea transformed astronomy forever.

Galileo Galilei improved the telescope and discovered Jupiter's four largest moons, proving that not everything revolves around Earth. Johannes Kepler later discovered that planets move in elliptical orbits.

🚀 Modern Astronomy

Modern astronomers use powerful observatories, satellites, and space telescopes to study the universe. Instruments such as the Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope allow scientists to observe galaxies billions of light-years away.

Astronomy now combines physics, mathematics, engineering, and computer science to explore everything from nearby planets to the birth of the universe.

📝 Key Takeaways

  • Astronomy began thousands of years ago through careful sky observations.
  • Ancient civilizations made important astronomical discoveries.
  • Copernicus, Galileo, and Kepler transformed our understanding of space.
  • Modern astronomy uses advanced technology to explore the universe.
